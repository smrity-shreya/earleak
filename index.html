Commercial angle: municipal water boards currently rely on manual leak inspection or customer complaints — both slow and reactive. EarLeak costs nothing to deploy (runs on phones citizens already own) and could be offered free to citizens while municipalities pay a small per-ward subscription for the prioritised repair queue, turning inspection cost into a data product.

The problem: most leak detection assumes you already own a smart water meter. Almost no household or municipal tap in India has one, so leaks run for weeks before anyone notices — usually via the bill.

The idea: a leaking tap, pipe or tank has a distinct acoustic signature — a steady high-frequency hiss, unlike the irregular burst of running water when someone actually uses the tap. EarLeak records 3 seconds of audio, measures signal energy, the proportion of energy in the hiss band, and how steady that signal stays over time, and turns that into a severity estimate and a litres/day loss figure.

The network: every scan a citizen runs — whether it finds a leak or confirms a tap is healthy — can be reported with a location, building a live city map. Municipal crews get a priority queue instead of a random complaint inbox, sorted by how much water is actually being wasted.

Prototype note: the classifier here runs a real-time audio feature analysis in your browser (no server, nothing uploaded). It's tuned on typical drip/leak acoustic patterns for this demo; a production version would train on a labelled field-recording dataset per plumbing type.
